# Full visual specification

## Canvas and mood

Create a horizontal 3:2 postcard on subtly rough warm ivory or cream paper. Combine the unchanged original photograph, minimal imperfect hand-drawn lines, clear flat acrylic color shapes, large intentional negative space, and restrained editorial point-line-plane composition.

The mood is quiet, warm, natural, playful, handmade, and collectible. Do not fill the background or add unrelated decoration.

## Adaptive layout

First determine the source orientation.

### Portrait source

- Use a left/right structure.
- Put the original photograph on the left at about 38–40% of total canvas width.
- Put the acrylic illustration in the upper-right.
- Put the divider and information row beneath the illustration on the right only.
- Leave a clearly visible, comfortable gap between the photo and right content.

### Landscape source

- Use a top/bottom structure.
- Put the original photograph at the top.
- Put the smaller acrylic illustration below it.
- Put the divider and information row beneath the illustration or in the associated negative-space area.
- Keep the two parts clearly separated and breathable.

## Photo treatment

Keep the original photograph real, sharp, and recognizable. Do not turn it into a drawing, painting, or filter treatment. Preserve identities, facial features, expressions, actions, clothing, and key objects. Crop only enough to support the layout while retaining the decisive subject and emotion.

Use a clean rectangular crop with no frame, border, tape, shadow, rounded corners, or decorative card.

## Illustration extraction

Retain only what is worth remembering:

- relationships between people
- the most distinctive action
- face direction and body posture
- hair and clothing silhouettes
- the decisive object
- the emotional instant

Delete complex backgrounds aggressively. Keep indoor lights, walls, furniture, toys, and clutter only when they are necessary for recognition. Outdoors, retain only a few representative shapes such as a branch, ridge, road, field, snow patch, lake stroke, or cloud.

Prefer too little detail over too much. Do not create a miniature realistic copy of the photo.

## Approved scale relationship

The photograph is the main memory; the illustration is a smaller emotional echo.

- Target an illustration visual area of about 60–70% of the visible photo area.
- Keep the illustration about 10–12% smaller than a conventional composition that would fill its content region.
- For a portrait source, let the illustration's widest span occupy about 70–75% of the right content region, never the entire right side.
- Keep substantial ivory paper visible above, below, left, and right of the illustration.
- Keep the illustration clear and substantial, not tiny, but never equal to or more dominant than the photograph.
- Do not shrink the photograph to manufacture balance.
- Do not enlarge text, swatches, or the divider to fill the resulting negative space.

## Irregular acrylic silhouette

Paint directly on the paper as a free irregular shape that follows the subject or scene silhouette. Use asymmetric contours, dry-brush texture, small breaks, uneven hand-painted edges, fading pigment, and exposed paper between color regions.

Never use a complete rectangle, square frame, regular background plate, pale rectangular wash, continuous border, or card-within-card effect.

## Lines

Use thin, light, concise, slightly unstable lines. Allow broken strokes, mild skew, varying thickness, incomplete contours, and selective omission. Lines suggest structure; color shapes establish the image.

Avoid engineering drawing, precise pen illustration, pure line art, commercial cartoon outlines, and fully closed contours.

## Acrylic color

Use confident, complete flat acrylic shapes with handmade coverage, light grain, uneven pigment, rough edges, and visible paper. Do not merely tint inside line work. Avoid watercolor bloom, colored pencil, crayon, smooth vector rendering, 3D, photorealistic painting, anime faces, and thick oil impasto.

Use no more than four main colors sampled and simplified from the original photo. Warm charcoal outline does not count. Let the paper provide most whites. Favor coordinated colors such as deep navy or charcoal, warm brown or brick red, cream, warm skin tone, mustard, or low-saturation green.

## Divider and information row

Place a very thin warm-gray divider beneath the illustration. Its length must match the illustration's widest visual span after all scaling. Leave comfortable air between illustration and divider.

Under the divider, place one information row:

- left: a three-digit series number
- center: one Chinese caption based on the photo
- right: exactly three rough acrylic color swatches sampled from the illustration

Keep all three parts on one visual baseline. Match the row's total width to the divider. Align the number's left edge to the divider's left end and the third swatch's right edge to the divider's right end. A short thin underline may sit below the number. Keep the row compact, elegant, and uncrowded.

Use a restrained Chinese Song/Ming serif or bookish thin serif in warm dark gray, never pure black.

## Caption

Write one photo-specific Chinese sentence of roughly 12–20 Chinese characters. Make it warm, natural, restrained, lightly poetic, and rooted in ordinary life. Do not exaggerate, moralize, explain the photo, use internet slang, imitate a headline, prepend a dash, or mechanically reuse an example. Keep it on one line.

## Text restrictions

Allow only the series number and one Chinese caption. Add no English, date, location, name, main title, subtitle, logo, watermark, decorative phrase, or extra symbol. Use exactly three swatches, no more and no fewer.

## Acceptance checklist

Before delivery confirm:

1. The layout matches the photo orientation.
2. The original photograph remains real, clear, and recognizable.
3. Identities, faces, actions, clothes, and key objects are preserved.
4. The illustration captures the decisive action, relationship, or object.
5. Irrelevant background is removed from the illustration.
6. The acrylic perimeter is genuinely irregular, not rectangular.
7. Paper grain and flat acrylic texture are visible.
8. Main colors do not exceed four.
9. Large intentional negative space remains.
10. The illustration is about 60–70% of the photo's visible area and does not overpower it.
11. The illustration remains clear rather than becoming tiny.
12. The divider matches the final illustration width.
13. Number, caption, and exactly three swatches share one baseline.
14. The information row matches the divider width.
15. The caption is photo-specific, natural, and one line.
16. No logo, watermark, English, date, or extra decoration appears.

The result should feel like a collectible postcard in which the real photograph preserves memory and the smaller paper-acrylic painting distills emotion.
